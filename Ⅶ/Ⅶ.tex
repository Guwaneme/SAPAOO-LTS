\section{Correctness and Parameter Analysis}
\subsection{Correctness}
\label{app:correctness-details}
Let $
\alpha=\frac{e^{1/5}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}.$
Probabilities in this correctness analysis include the
sampling of $(s,e)$ during key generation as well as preprocessing.
We use the standard subgaussian norm bound for the ideal discrete-Gaussian
samples and apply it directly to independent sums; no convolution is replaced
by an exact spherical discrete Gaussian. {Concretely, for
independent centered samples $x_k$ with subgaussian parameters $\sigma_k$, the
coefficientwise moment-generating-function bound and independence give
subgaussian parameter $(\sum_k\sigma_k^2)^{1/2}$ for $\sum_kx_k$; the standard
vector-norm tail bound with
$\alpha=\frac{e^{1/5}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}.$ and $N\geq16\lambda$ is at most
$2^{-\lambda}$.} In particular, for
$a=|\mathsf{act}|\leq a_{\max}$, except with probability at most
$6\cdot2^{-\lambda}$, all six bounds
\[
\begin{gathered}
\left\|\sum_jy_j\right\|_2
\leq\alpha\sqrt{aN}\,\sigma_2,
\qquad
\left\|\sum_je'_j\right\|_2
\leq\alpha\sqrt{aN}\,\sigma_2,\\
\left\|\sum_jr_{j,1}\right\|_2,
\left\|\sum_jr_{j,2}\right\|_2
\leq\alpha\sqrt{aN}\,\sigma_r,\\
\|s\|_2,\|e\|_2
\leq\alpha\sqrt N\,\sigma_1
\end{gathered}
\]
hold simultaneously when $N\geq16\lambda$. The indices in these sums range over
$\mathsf{act}$. Since $\|c\|_1=\tau$, Young's inequality for negacyclic
convolution gives
\[
\|sc\|_2\leq\tau\|s\|_2,
\qquad
\|ec\|_2\leq\tau\|e\|_2.
\]
This avoids treating $sc$ or $ec$ as an independent spherical Gaussian.

To support every active set of size at most $a_{\max}$, define the conservative
bounds
\[
\begin{aligned}
\beta_z(a_{\max})
&=
\alpha\sqrt N
\left(\sqrt{a_{\max}}\,\sigma_2+\tau\sigma_1\right),\\
\beta_r(a_{\max})
&=
\alpha\sqrt{a_{\max}N}\,\sigma_r,\\
\beta_{\widetilde e}(a_{\max})
&=
\alpha\sqrt N
\left(
\sqrt{a_{\max}}(\sigma_2+\sigma_r)
+\tau\sigma_1
\right)
+\frac{q\tau}{2q_1}\sqrt N,\\
\beta_\Delta(a_{\max})
&=
\frac{q_2}{q}\beta_{\widetilde e}(a_{\max})+\sqrt N.
\end{aligned}
\]
The verifier uses
$\beta_z=\beta_z(a_{\max})$,
$\beta_r=\beta_r(a_{\max})$, and
$\beta_\Delta=\beta_\Delta(a_{\max})$.

For an honest execution, pairwise-mask cancellation and Shamir reconstruction
give
\[
\begin{aligned}
z
&=
\sum_{j\in\mathsf{act}}
\left(y_j+\lambda_{\mathsf{act},j}s_jc+m_j\right)\\
&=
\sum_{j\in\mathsf{act}}y_j+sc,
\qquad
r=\sum_{j\in\mathsf{act}}r_{j,2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Let
\[
\eta_1
=
\widehat b-\mathsf{Lift}_1(b),
\qquad
\|\eta_1\|_\infty\leq\frac{q}{2q_1},
\]
and define the residual, using canonical centered representatives in
$\mathcal M_q$, by
\[
\widetilde e
=
\sum_{j\in\mathsf{act}}(e'_j+r_{j,1})
+ec-\eta_1c.
\]
For
$X=az+h_{\mathsf{sid}}r-\mathsf{Lift}_1(bc_{\mathbb Z})$, where the product
is formed in $R_{q_1}$ before lifting, the honest aggregate token
satisfies
\[
C_{\mathsf{act}}
=
\left\lfloor X+\widetilde e\right\rceil_{p_2}.
\]
The bounds above and
$\|\eta_1c\|_2\leq(q\tau/(2q_1))\sqrt N$ imply
$\|\widetilde e\|_2\leq\beta_{\widetilde e}(a_{\max})$. The modulus-switching
difference bound then gives
\[
\|\Delta\|_2
\leq
\frac{q_2}{q}\|\widetilde e\|_2+\sqrt N
\leq
\beta_\Delta(a_{\max}).
\]
Finally, the definition of $\Delta$ yields
\[
C'
=
\Delta+\lfloor X\rceil_{p_2}
=
C_{\mathsf{act}},
\]
so the verifier recomputes the honest challenge.
% BEGIN ROUTE A REVISION (2026-09-24)
In this construction, at least one honest signer belongs to every admissible active
set because $|\mathcal C|<t\leq|\mathsf{act}|$. A successful honest signing
execution has therefore bound the exact context before its response
collection completes. The persistent binding read matches the context
reconstructed above. Hence the added check introduces no further
cryptographic correctness error with the ideal available service.
An implementation's probability of an unavailable or invalid read must be
accounted for separately; correctness does not promise progress after
adversarial burns that prevent a signing execution from completing.
% END ROUTE A REVISION

A union bound over the six
displayed subgaussian events gives
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathsf{corr}}^{\mathsf{pt}}(\lambda)
\leq
6\cdot2^{-\lambda}+\varepsilon_{\mathsf{samp}}(\lambda),
\]
where $\varepsilon_{\mathsf{samp}}$ is the joint statistical distance of an
implemented Gaussian sampler from the ideal samples and is zero in the ideal
distributional model. In the pointwise bound, this joint distribution contains
$(s,e)$ and all samples used by the fixed active set. In the uniform bound, it
contains $(s,e)$ and the complete preprocessing family for every signer from
which the active set may be selected. If only per-sample distances are
available, $\varepsilon_{\mathsf{samp}}$ includes their composition over the
corresponding batch. {A concrete implementation is
required to make $\varepsilon_{\mathsf{samp}}(\lambda)$ negligible. This is a
pointwise bound for each fixed admissible $\mathsf{act}$ chosen independently
of the preprocessing randomness. For active-set selection after observing the
public tokens, let
\[
S(n,t,a_{\max})
=
\sum_{a=t}^{a_{\max}}\binom{n}{a}.
\]
For each admissible $\mathsf{act}$, let
$(\sigma_{\mathsf{act}},\mathcal B_{\mathsf{act}})$ be the result of an
honest counterfactual execution from a separate copy of the same fresh
post-preprocessing state, using the common sampled key and token family.
Each copy binds only its own context. The event below means that every
such choice would succeed if selected; only one choice is executed in the
real protocol. A union bound on the Gaussian norm events before choosing
the active set gives the uniform event
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\Pr\!\left[
\begin{array}{c}
\forall\mathsf{act}\subseteq[n],\quad
t\leq|\mathsf{act}|\leq a_{\max}:\\
\mathsf{Vf}^{\mathcal B_{\mathsf{act}}}
(\mathsf{vk},\mathsf{msg},\sigma_{\mathsf{act}})=1
\end{array}
\right]\\
&\qquad\geq
1-6S(n,t,a_{\max})2^{-\lambda}
-\varepsilon_{\mathsf{samp}}(\lambda).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus adaptive post-preprocessing selection uses
$\varepsilon_{\mathsf{corr}}^{\mathsf{unif}}
\leq6S(n,t,a_{\max})2^{-\lambda}+\varepsilon_{\mathsf{samp}}$.
The supported parameter regime must restrict $(n,t,a_{\max},N)$ so that the
chosen pointwise or uniform error target is negligible.}

\paragraph{Exact verification thresholds}
Use $\alpha=e^{1/5}/\sqrt{2\pi}$ and the preceding correctness formulas
to obtain ideal bounds $\beta_z^{\rm ideal}$, $\beta_r^{\rm ideal}$,
and $\beta_\Delta^{\rm ideal}$. Round their squares outwards:
\[
K_z=\lceil(\beta_z^{\rm ideal})^2\rceil,\quad
K_r=\lceil(\beta_r^{\rm ideal})^2\rceil,\quad
K_\Delta=\lceil(\beta_\Delta^{\rm ideal})^2\rceil.
\]
The exact constants are
\[
\begin{aligned}
K_z&=1203919592477717581798617694083,\\
K_r&=4592588776922465877528417,\\
K_\Delta&=142861.
\end{aligned}
\]
The verifier compares the integer squared norms with these integers.
For this profile, use the actual radii
$\beta_z=\sqrt{K_z}$, $\beta_r=\sqrt{K_r}$, and
$\beta_\Delta=\sqrt{K_\Delta}$ throughout extraction. Numerically,
\[
\begin{aligned}

\paragraph{Strict width checks and correctness}
We use the strict Hint-RLWE relation in TalonG's Theorem 1, retaining
$\tau^2$ rather than replacing the relaxed asymptotic factor by one:
\[
\sigma=\left(\frac{2}{\sigma_1^2}
               +\frac{2Q\tau^2}{\sigma_2^2}\right)^{-1/2}
\simeq20.2385770251.
\]
A sufficient upper bound on $\sqrt2\eta_\epsilon(\mathbb Z^N)$ is
$\sqrt{2\ln(2N(1+1/\epsilon))/\pi}\simeq12.1220175590$.
The selected ephemeral and preimage widths satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_2&>2\sqrt N q^{1/2+1/N}

\sigma_r&>1.17\sqrt q\sqrt{\ln(4N(1+1/\epsilon))/\pi}
                 \simeq1.3480523948\cdot10^9.

\]

\paragraph{Interpretation of heuristic estimates}
The cost models and generic coefficient-lattice mappings above are used
as explicitly heuristic evidence about known attacks. They are not
claimed as proven lower bounds against all algorithms. Such estimates
can guide a candidate parameter choice without a tight concrete security
reduction, provided that the instance distribution, attack coverage,
cost model, and extrapolations are stated. In contrast, simulation
identities, extraction bounds, and statistical-distance composition in
the conditional theorem require mathematical justification. An
unproved primitive property must be identified as an assumption rather
than inferred from a favorable estimator output. In particular, the
omitted NTRU estimate leaves this profile without a complete numerical
assessment of all assumptions used by the proof.
% END BLUE HEURISTIC-SCOPE CLARIFICATION

\paragraph{Additional screens and communication}
The challenge entropy is
$\tau+\log_2\binom N\tau\simeq298.1615854$ bits. The correction radius
is strictly below $(q_2/2)\sqrt N$. For only the fresh, uniform-$C$,
$z=r=0$ strategy with a fresh challenge-oracle input, the finite-alphabet Chernoff bound is
less than $2^{-30272}$ per trial. This does not bound a fixed or
adversarially selected $C$, or general forgeries.
Simple fixed-width encoding uses 51, 42, and 10 bits per coefficient
of $z,r,\Delta$, respectively. Their total is 52,736 bytes (51.5 KiB),
excluding the challenge and metadata. Encoding full $a$ and compressed
$b$ takes 35 KiB; one uncompressed public token polynomial takes 27 KiB.
A canonical sparse challenge representation takes 52 bytes. These are
encoding budgets, not measured protocol bandwidth or runtime results.

\paragraph{Remaining proof and implementation obligations}
The state-assisted theorem eliminates $E_1$ in the ideal service model,
but its $E_0$ term still incurs label-guessing and forking losses. No
end-to-end 128-bit claim follows from the estimator outputs alone. Such
a concrete claim requires explicit experiment-wide budgets, including
$L,T$, PRF and sampler errors, supported primitive advantage bounds,
and a realization of the trusted service. A trapdoor implementation must supply the required guarantees at
this modulus and dimension. Changing the supported participant sets,
query budget, or widths requires recomputation of both correctness and
security conditions.
